# Gray and Van Bavel: purposive full-text diagnostic audit

This AI-assisted audit verifies the identities of Kurt Gray (Scopus author ID 15520639300) and Jay J. Van Bavel (22986966200), then examines four eligible articles per researcher. These examples were selected independently from their publications to diagnose coverage; they are not a probability sample or an estimate of query recall or precision. Original abstracts and downloaded PDFs remain private. Article years in the tables use Scopus metadata, which can differ from the final issue year.

Identity was checked against an exact publication, its byline, and institutional affiliation. The [Ohio State faculty page](https://psychology.osu.edu/people/gray.1750) identifies Gray and links his publication site; his [PNAS article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8017692/) verifies his earlier UNC affiliation. Van Bavel’s [current professional page](https://www.jayvanbavel.com/) and the [Cambridge institutional record of his Nature Human Behaviour article](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/d821cd31-d1e1-4798-86db-5da0c63a83e1) corroborate the NYU psychologist and ORCID. This disambiguation matters because a different Jay van Bavel publishes allergy research. Complete journal-Article bibliographies for 2010–2026 contain 114 Scopus records for Gray and 106 for Van Bavel; all 220 have structurally complete author sequences. Bibliographies are unscreened raw records, not confirmed experiments or independent datasets.

The proposed clause adds one confirmed eligible first/last-author article for each researcher: Gray’s [Equating silence with violence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2022.104348) randomizes anti-racist message versions and measures threat and resistance; Van Bavel’s [Identity concerns drive belief](https://doi.org/10.1177/13684302211030004) varies partisan quotations and news and measures belief and sharing intentions. Both abstracts contain the procedural language requested by the clause.

All eight examined articles are in the journal frame. Only those two match the new clause, so the other examples demonstrate wording and metadata limitations rather than journal exclusions. Gray’s personal-experience and threat-acknowledgment papers report eligible vignette experiments in their methods but summarize the work using study or intervention language. His algorithmic-discrimination paper likewise summarizes eight studies without the required nearby reading/manipulation phrase. Van Bavel’s moral-responsibility abstract says participants learned about an event, while the full methods describe reading manipulated scenarios. His political-devotion article reports fact-checking and accuracy nudges but lacks the required proximity construction. His accuracy-motivation article is already found by the previous broader candidate query, yet is missed by the new clause and gives Van Bavel no first/last-author credit because he is third of four authors. Source links and study-level explanations are in `author_fulltext_examples.csv`.

Seven have explicit evidence of US participants, including two with additional countries; the moral-responsibility paper remains geographically unclear because Mechanical Turk alone does not establish country. Questionnaire compatibility remains unverified for all eight: repeated stimuli, graphics, branching, or mixed study types need inspection of the actual questionnaire and data. Eligibility does not establish current possession of usable data.
