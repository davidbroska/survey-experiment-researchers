Code the **article**, retaining evidence about at least one qualifying study. Search phrases identify candidates; they do not establish eligibility.

| Variable | Coding rule |
|---|---|
| `survey_experiment` | `yes` if the article reports experimentally assigned survey treatments or profiles and self-reported judgments, beliefs, intentions, or choices; `no` for clear nonexperiments or experiments conducted outside the survey; otherwise `unclear`. |
| `new_data` | `yes` if the authors report fielding the relevant experimental data; `no` for methods-only discussion, synthesis, or secondary analysis without new fielding; otherwise `unclear`. Author contribution or methods statements may be needed. |
| `parser_compatible` | `yes` if the treatment assignment and response paths can be reconstructed by the project's parser from the survey instrument and export; `no` for an unsupported design; otherwise `unclear`. Verify the actual instrument when metadata are insufficient. |
| `data_access` | `yes` if there is evidence that the researchers can supply respondent-level data and the corresponding survey and treatment materials for donation; `no` if they cannot; otherwise `unclear`. Record the basis in `notes` and `source_url`. |
| `panel_provider` | Record the recruitment provider when established. A provider name is not evidence of access to data, and an absent provider name is not an exclusion. |
| `text_treatment` | `yes` if wording, written information, or a written profile is manipulated in at least one qualifying study, including mixed-modality articles with a genuine text manipulation; `no` if only images, video, audio, physical interventions, or other nontext components vary; otherwise `unclear`. |
| `evidence_field`, `evidence_quote` | Record the source field and a verbatim supporting span. Full-text evidence must refer to a saved `private/fulltext/SCOPUS_ID.txt` file. The pipeline verifies every positive design quote. |
| `reviewer`, `reviewer_type` | Record the actual reviewer and `human` or `AI_assisted`; never treat machine coding as independently verified human coding. |
| `source_url`, `notes` | Identify supplementary evidence, unresolved features, mixed studies, or the reason for exclusion. |

An experiment need not use a representative sample or a US sample. Recruitment prioritizes researchers with accessible data and instruments from their own survey projects, including studies recruited through Bovitz, Prolific, and other online panels. Data access and parser compatibility must both be established before an article qualifies for the reviewed donor ranking. A factorial label alone does not establish compatibility: verify how conditions, profiles, and response paths were implemented. A drug trial followed by a satisfaction questionnaire is not made into a survey experiment by that questionnaire. Recruitment interventions outside the survey fall outside the treatment definition. Random sampling is not random treatment assignment. Do not infer participants' nationality from their authors' affiliations.

The query has no negative keyword filters. Mentions of designs that may need closer inspection are recorded as `design_review_cue`; this flag never excludes an article automatically. Compatibility is a study-level judgment based on the actual design. The `panel_provider_cues` and author-level `n_panel_cue_articles` fields record textual mentions only, not verified recruitment channels or confirmed data possession.

Text classification concerns **what varies**, rather than everything a respondent encounters. A leaflet with identical text and varying illustrations is not a text treatment. Vignettes may be written, spoken, or filmed. Absent information stays `unclear`; it is not coded `no`.

Use [article_decisions.csv](inputs/article_decisions.csv) for eligibility judgments. `analyse` incorporates valid decisions and exports a ranking of the reviewed subset separately. It never labels that subset as a completed invitation ranking. Review the articles supporting the 100-person pool, cutoff ties, and unresolved database matches before finalizing the invitation list. Researchers can have split or merged Scopus profiles; corrections need a documented crosswalk rather than name-only aggregation.

The frozen validation queue uses disjoint strata: exact core first, then the first matching expansion in the archived family order, then unverified database matches. Within each stratum, a fixed SHA-256 ordering selects up to 40 core records or 25 other records. The files preserve population size, sample size, and inclusion probability. Two reviewers should code independently and resolve disagreements, retaining unclear cases. `validate.py` computes stratum summaries and weights by population size rather than pooling the deliberately disproportionate sample. Its weighted yes-to-yes-or-unclear range concerns unresolved classifications and is not a confidence interval. This follow-up queue can overlap development material; it is not an untouched holdout. A credible recall study would additionally require positives identified independently of these search terms and reserved before vocabulary development.

Development annotations stored privately concern broad survey-experiment design and treatment modality. They do not establish data access, parser compatibility, identity, or donor eligibility and are not copied into the article-decision table.
# Researcher affiliations and sample geography

`inputs/researcher_affiliations.csv` is the current affiliation table keyed by Scopus `authid`. It records full name, institution, department or unit, institution/campus country, role, source URL, verification date, source status, and explanatory notes. Country does not describe citizenship or participant location. Visiting or courtesy roles do not automatically replace the primary work affiliation. Dated sources and conflicting role descriptions are flagged explicitly.

`inputs/article_geography.csv` contains 910 article-level metadata annotations across the current and previous cohorts keyed by `scopus_id`. `inputs/dashboard_config.json` sets the 100-person pool, cutoff-tie policy, and download batch size. The pool is selected before geography coding using total first/last-author candidate article counts. These are AI-assisted annotations, not independently human-validated labels.

| Field or label | Definition |
|---|---|
| `us_explicit` | Title, abstract, or available keywords directly identifies a US experimental population/setting or findings attributed to Americans. Named US states or cities can provide explicit geographic evidence. |
| `us_inferred` | US sampling is plausible from country-specific study context without a direct sampling statement. Record the contextual inference and its limits. An explicitly foreign sample assessing US policy remains non-US. |
| `non_us` | Explicit or strongly contextual non-US study evidence; no US experimental sample identified in the reviewed metadata. This does not establish that the full article contains none. |
| `unclear` | Metadata does not locate the experimental sample. Unspecified national or multinational samples, vendors, generic topics, and author affiliations do not establish geography. A US observational sample does not establish that a separate experiment sampled Americans. |
| `not_applicable` | Metadata identifies no applicable original experimental sample, e.g. methodological commentary or a synthesis of existing studies. Does not automatically change the frozen provisional candidate count. |
| `evidence_field`, `evidence_quote`, `rationale` | Field is `title`, `abstract`, `keywords`, or `indexed_keywords`; every non-unclear label requires a matching verbatim span. All records require a rationale. Indexed keywords were unavailable in the archived Search COMPLETE records. |
| `us_and_non_us_samples_reported` | Article with US evidence also reports non-US samples. Count once in US totals. With an inferred label, this flag inherits the inference's uncertainty. |
| `metadata_sha256` | SHA-256 of a UTF-8 JSON object containing title, abstract, author keywords, and indexed keywords, with sorted keys and Unicode preserved. Any change invalidates the annotation. |
| `reviewer`, `reviewer_type`, `review_date`, `human_validated` | Actual reviewer and date; machine review is `AI_assisted` and does not establish human validation. |

`results/top100_enriched.csv` joins these data to the frozen ranking. The highlighted **Survey-experiment articles** column is `n_articles = n_first + n_last - n_sole`; both first and last include sole authors. A shared article counts once for each first/last author and once in the article-level denominator. Multiple experiments in one article receive one article credit.

The five `n_<label>_articles` columns partition each author's credited articles. **US-sample articles**, `n_us_articles`, sums explicit and inferred evidence from all available metadata fields. Mixed-country counts overlap with this total and must not be added again. `us_count_rank_within_pool` is a competition rank (equal counts share rank) among the selected researchers. `us_if_all_unclear_resolve_us` adds all unclear articles to the current US count as a sensitivity scenario, not a confidence limit; existing inferred or non-US labels may also change.

The 930 first/last/sole author–article links are in `results/top100_author_article_links.csv`; the 870 distinct articles and evidence are in `results/top100_article_annotations.csv`. These count publications, not independent samples, datasets, or verified data holdings. Geographic review remains separate from `inputs/article_decisions.csv` eligibility review. A positive keyword match can retrieve a design that the parser cannot handle; no negative design keyword filter is applied.

# Full-text review round

`results/fulltext_download_queue.csv` lists each unclear paper once. Priority sorts by number of credited pool researchers, highest total count among them, highest current US count among them, and numeric article ID. This is a review-efficiency rule, not a US-sample prediction. Batches and suggested filenames support the manual download process.

Save `<scopus_id>.pdf` in `private/fulltext/inbox/`; supplements can be `<scopus_id>__supplement.pdf`. `python3 pipeline/fulltext.py` writes an inventory, page-level text caches, and a blank review template under `private/fulltext/`. Text extraction alone never changes a label. Page numbers refer to PDF file pages starting at 1, not printed journal pagination. UTF-8 `.txt` files are treated as a one-page source.

Record reviewed decisions in `inputs/fulltext_reviews.csv`. Required fields are `scopus_id`, resolved `sample_us_label`, `filename`, `source_sha256`, `page`, `evidence_quote`, `rationale`, `us_and_non_us_samples_reported`, and the reviewer provenance fields. One final decision per article is allowed. A non-US decision requires checking all relevant experimental samples; one non-US study does not rule out a US sample in another study. Evidence must match the cited page in the extracted text and the file hash must match the supplied PDF. Unresolved articles stay in the queue. Reviews can revise inferred or non-US labels too if the full text becomes available.

Running `dashboard` or `reports` applies valid full-text overrides to every credited researcher and removes resolved articles from the unclear queue. The original metadata input remains unchanged; output fields retain `metadata_sample_us_label`, `source_review`, `fulltext_filename`, `fulltext_page`, and `fulltext_source_sha256`. AI-assisted full-text review must retain `human_validated=false` until independently checked by a human. Download checkboxes in the browser are separate convenience records and are never treated as evidence.

The earlier `inputs/top40_*` and `results/top40_*` CSV files are retained as historical snapshots; their abstract-only and title-only fields are not the current dashboard's counting definition.

`results/year_window_tie_comparison.csv` compares count distributions in six scenarios that separate year window, pool membership, and full-text overrides. `tied_pairs` is the number of unordered researcher pairs sharing a count, out of `all_pairs`; it does not treat a secondary display sort as substantive tie-breaking. `year_window_author_comparison.csv` holds the current 100 researchers fixed under both year windows. Original annotations and profiles outside the current pool remain in the canonical inputs for reproducibility.


**PDF availability and sample evidence.** Availability is independent of geography. `open_access_acquisition.csv` records retrieval status, stable source URL, filename, SHA-256 hash, verification result, and acquisition notes. `fulltext_download_queue.csv` retains every article with unclear geography. `local_pdf_available` reflects a PDF currently present in the shared inbox, including a separately named supplement; it does not establish that the methods resolve sample country. The manual-only queue uses `manual_position` and `manual_batch`, distinct from the complete review inventory's original positions. Indexed open locations that could not be fetched remain eligible for manual retrieval. No additional full-text label is created by the acquisition stage; reviewed decisions require the existing provenance fields in `inputs/fulltext_reviews.csv`.


**Tie-directed download priorities.** `top50_priority_downloads.csv` contains missing articles with possible US-count differentiation among competition ranks 1–50, including all boundary ties. Pair counts simulate US-positive classification while giving every credited author one additional US article simultaneously. Total-count ties are considered distinguishable only through the separate US evidence measure; total counts never change. `new_top50_us_tied_pairs_if_us` records newly created US ties. Ten-paper batches greedily cover different tied pairs; effects are conditional scenarios, not expected effects or US-sample probabilities. `manual_retrieval_status=user_unavailable` excludes an article from priority requests but retains it in the full inventory. Abstract-only PDFs do not count as usable full texts.
