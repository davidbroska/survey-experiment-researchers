# Audit of user-supplied counterexamples

Reviewed 10 Gordon Pennycook and 10 Jennifer A. Richeson papers against publisher pages, author manuscripts, and institutional repositories on 10 September 2026. These were supplied to challenge the search and are a development set, not an independent validation sample. The accompanying CSV records study-design evidence, source location, byline position, geography uncertainty, and unresolved parser requirements. These AI-assisted judgments require human validation. This audit does not change the ranking. Apart from the documented document-type exception below, retrieval membership and each selection gate are audited separately.

Five Pennycook examples credit him as first author; five have him in a middle position. Nine Richeson examples credit her as last author; the Kachanoff paper has her fourth of five. First/last counting therefore explains some, but cannot by itself explain all, of these researchers' absences. Richeson's institutional CV also records a 2012 TESS award with Craig ([Yale CV](https://psychology.yale.edu/sites/default/files/richesoncv9_24.pdf), p.4).

A document-type error is also material: Scopus indexes *Majority No More?* (2018), EID 2-s2.0-85056872453, as `re` / Review. The 10 September DOI lookup therefore establishes exclusion by `DOCTYPE(ar)`, rather than absence from Scopus. Its full text nevertheless reports randomized participant assignments and empirical results in Studies 3–4 ([author manuscript](https://spcl.yale.edu/media/138/download?inline=), pp.8–12). This is evidence of experimental content, not evidence that its datasets are independent of previous papers.

The substantive problem is variation in how designs are described. Clear experimental work can be summarized as studies that manipulate information, expose people to materials, or compare responses after different reading assignments, without using a survey-experiment label. Sometimes the abstract omits even experiment or randomization terminology. Thus adding participant* is useful but cannot repair all missing cases. Fixed phrases also miss active voice and intervening words; a requirement that population and design cues appear in one sentence can discard evidence distributed across title and abstract.

The supplied cases are not all equivalent positive benchmarks. Callaghan et al. used an in-person laboratory intervention with videos and interviewer listening, followed by survey measurements ([author manuscript](https://spcl.yale.edu/media/123/download?inline=), p.6). Pennycook et al.'s 2024 intervention includes video and multiple factors; the abstract alone cannot establish parser compatibility. Other papers mix survey experiments with field or correlational studies. Eligibility should be based on at least one qualifying study, with modality and compatibility coded separately. A publication is not an independent dataset.

Geography was rechecked conservatively: 13 articles have explicit US evidence after primary-source review, one has a contextual inference from participants reporting their own US presidential vote, and six remain unclear. US topics, generic partisan preferences, racial response categories, institutional affiliations, and online vendors alone do not establish US residence. These source-review labels must not be mistaken for abstract-only annotations.

A defensible strategy separates candidate retrieval from eligibility confirmation. Freeze a short design-based search, then evaluate it on an independently sampled set of retrieved and potentially missed records across fields. Screen for an investigator-controlled stimulus variation, a human survey-response outcome, and a link between the two in the same study. Where indexed fields are inadequate, use full text; retain unresolved cases explicitly. National representativeness and sample vendors should be described, not used as evidence of data ownership. No individual names, research topics, or institutional affiliations should enter the retrieval rule.

The source audit supports a generalizable search revision and an explicit screening stage. It does not estimate sensitivity or precision, guarantee error-free retrieval, establish that every study in an article is supported by the parser, or justify promoting the named researchers without the same eligibility and counting rules applied to everyone.
